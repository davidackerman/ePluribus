Wait, how does this work?

Every piece is a square crop of a real science image, stretched to cover the whole frame. The computer adds them one at a time, always picking the crop whose pattern of light and dark best fills in what's still missing, then re-tunes the brightness of every piece so far.

Pieces can only add light, never take it away, like hundreds of slide projectors pointed at one screen. So the stack is a bright glow with the face as a faint ripple on top. One black-level setting, the same for every pixel, removes the glow.

One brightness per image. No masks, no per-pixel tricks: just whole images, added up. "32% Moon" is each source's share of the brightness × contrast that draws the picture.
