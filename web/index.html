Wait, how does this work?

Every piece is a square crop of a real image (a map, a telescope shot, a microscope slide, a painting), stretched to cover the whole frame. The computer adds them one at a time, always picking the crop whose pattern of light and dark best fills in what's still missing, then re-tunes the brightness of every piece so far.

Pieces can only add light, never take it away, like hundreds of slide projectors pointed at one screen. So the stack is a bright glow with the picture as a faint ripple on top. One black-level setting, the same for every pixel, removes the glow.

One brightness per image. No masks, no per-pixel tricks: just whole images, added up. "32% Moon" is each source's share of the brightness × contrast that draws the picture.

    About: ePluribus turns any picture into a picture made of 300 other pictures. It's not a mosaic: instead of tiling small photos side by side, every image covers the whole frame. It layers 300 whole images (Mars and Moon maps, Hubble and Webb galaxies and nebulae, microscope slides, Earth from orbit, public-domain paintings) like a 300-image multiple exposure, adding their light until they make your picture. Free, and it runs entirely in your browser.
